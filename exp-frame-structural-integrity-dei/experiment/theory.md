# Theoretical Derivations — Frame Structural Integrity

This document details the governing physical principles, centroid math, and beam mechanics equations used to evaluate drone balancing and structural load limits.

---

## 1. Centroid & Center of Gravity (Sub-Calc A)
To ensure hover stability, the drone's center of mass must align with its geometric center (origin). In a Cartesian coordinate system, the Center of Gravity (CG) coordinates along the lateral ($x$) and longitudinal ($y$) axes are calculated using the centroid equations:

$$x_{\mathrm{CG}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (m_i \cdot x_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i}$$

$$y_{\mathrm{CG}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (m_i \cdot y_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i}$$

Where:
* $m_i$ is the mass of the individual component (g or kg).
* $x_i, y_i$ are the coordinates of the component's center of mass relative to the geometric center (mm or m).

The total radial offset of the center of gravity ($r_{\mathrm{CG}}$) from the datum origin is solved using the Euclidean distance:

$$r_{\mathrm{CG}} = \sqrt{x_{\mathrm{CG}}^2 + y_{\mathrm{CG}}^2}$$

To prevent asymmetric motor heating and hover drift, we enforce the safety envelope:
$$r_{\mathrm{CG}} \le 10\text{ mm}$$

## 2. Cantilever Beam Bending Moment (Sub-Calc B)
Each of the four arms of the quadcopter acts as a cantilever beam—fixed at the central hub plate (root clamp) and free at the outer motor end (tip load). Under hover or full-throttle conditions, the forces acting on the arm tip ($F_{\mathrm{tip}}$) are composed of:
1. **Gravitational Weight:** The combined mass of the motor ($M_{\mathrm{motor}}$) and propeller ($M_{\mathrm{prop}}$).
2. **Aerodynamic Thrust:** The upward reaction force ($T_{\mathrm{max}}$) generated by the propeller.

$$F_{\mathrm{tip}} = (M_{\mathrm{motor}} + M_{\mathrm{prop}}) \cdot g + T_{\mathrm{max}}$$

Where $g = 9.80665\text{ m/s}^2$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

The bending moment $M(x)$ at any distance $x$ from the root clamp is the product of the force and the remaining span length:

$$M(x) = -F_{\mathrm{tip}} \cdot (L - x)$$

The maximum bending moment occurs at the clamped root ($x = 0$):

$$M_{\mathrm{max}} = -F_{\mathrm{tip}} \cdot L$$

Where $L$ is the total arm span length (meters). The shear force $V(x)$ remains constant along the entire span:
$$V(x) = -F_{\mathrm{tip}}$$

---

## 3. Flexural Bending Stress (Sub-Calc C)
The applied bending moment creates tensile stress on the upper fibers of the arm and compression stress on the lower fibers. The normal bending stress ($\sigma$) at any vertical distance $y$ from the horizontal Neutral Axis is solved using the flexure formula:

$$\sigma(y) = \frac{M \cdot y}{I}$$

The maximum bending stress ($\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}$) occurs at the outermost boundary fibers ($y = \pm c$, where $c = h/2$):

$$\sigma_{\mathrm{max}} = \frac{M_{\mathrm{max}} \cdot c}{I} = \frac{M_{\mathrm{max}} \cdot h}{2 I}$$

### Hollow Rectangular Area Moment of Inertia ($I$)
Quadcopter arms are typically manufactured as hollow tubes to save weight while maintaining stiffness. For a hollow rectangular cross-section of width $b$, height $h$, and wall thickness $t$, the area moment of inertia is derived by subtracting the void area from the outer rectangular envelope:

$$I = I_{\mathrm{outer}} - I_{\mathrm{inner}} = \frac{b \cdot h^3}{12} - \frac{(b - 2t) \cdot (h - 2t)^3}{12}$$

---

## 4. Safety Factor & Structural Deflection (Sub-Calc D)
To determine if the selected arm material will yield under load, the maximum applied stress is compared to the material's yield strength ($\sigma_{\mathrm{yield}}$). The Safety Factor ($SF$) is defined as:

$$SF = \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{yield}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{applied}}}$$

*   **$SF \ge 2.0$ (Safe):** Strong structural headroom.
*   **$1.0 \le SF < 2.0$ (Marginal):** High risk of structural fatigue or permanent deformation under dynamic flight loads.
*   **$SF < 1.0$ (Fail):** The applied stress exceeds the yield point, causing immediate mechanical failure (yielding/fracture).

### Elastic Deflection Profile
The vertical deflection shape of the arm ($y(x)$) as a function of distance $x$ from the clamp is modeled using the Euler-Bernoulli beam deflection equation:

$$y(x) = \frac{F_{\mathrm{tip}} \cdot x^2}{6 E I} (3L - x)$$

Where:
* $E$ is the material's Young's Modulus (GPa), representing stiffness.
* $I$ is the area moment of inertia ($m^4$).
* $x$ is the position along the span ($0 \le x \le L$).

This equation explains why Nylon (low Young's Modulus $E = 3.0\text{ GPa}$) experiences extreme deflection and yielding compared to Carbon Fibre ($E = 150.0\text{ GPa}$), which remains highly rigid under equivalent loads.
