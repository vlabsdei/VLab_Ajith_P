Propulsion System Design: Propeller Geometry, Thrust & Motor Matching

Theoretical Background: Multirotor Propulsion Systems

A multirotor's propulsion system converts electrical energy stored in a chemical battery into aerodynamic thrust. Designing an efficient and safe drone requires understanding the coupled physics of aerodynamics, electro-circuit kinetics, thermodynamics, and battery chemistry.

Before working through the underlying physics, the animation below presents an exploded view of a complete propulsion unit. It separates the carbon-fibre frame, the BLDC motor, the propeller, the battery, and the avionics stack so each component can be identified before it is analysed in the sections that follow.

1. Aerodynamic Force Balance & Hover State

For a multirotor to maintain a stationary hover in three-dimensional space, the net vertical force acting on the aircraft must be zero. The total thrust generated by the Nmotors propellers must balance the gravity load (weight) of the drone:

Σ Fy = Nmotors · T - Mtotal · g = 0

Trequired = (Mtotal · g) ⁄ Nmotors

Where:

  • Trequired is the static thrust required per motor-propeller unit [N].
  • Mtotal is the total All-Up Mass (AUM) of the quadcopter, including the frame, electronics, battery, propulsion units, and any payload [kg].
  • g is the acceleration due to gravity (9.80665 m/s2).
  • Nmotors is the number of motors (typically 4 for standard quadcopters).

Worked Example — Hover Thrust

Consider the coherent 5-inch build used throughout this chapter: a 250 mm carbon-fibre frame (cf_250, 68.2 g), four 2204-2300KV motors (25.0 g each), four 5045 propellers (3.1 g each), a 4S 2200 mAh LiPo (4s_2200, 162.0 g), four 30 A ESCs (esc_30a_blheli32, 9.5 g each), an F4 flight controller (6.2 g) and an ELRS receiver (3.5 g). Summing these masses gives an All-Up Mass of Mtotal = 390.3 g ≈ 0.390 kg. Substituting into the hover balance:

Trequired = (0.390 kg · 9.80665 m⁄s2) ⁄ 4 ≈ 0.957 N

Each motor-propeller unit must therefore generate about 0.957 N (≈ 97.6 gf) of static thrust to hold a hover — exactly one quarter of the 3.83 N total weight.

2. Propeller Aerodynamics (Blade Element Momentum Theory)

Aerodynamic thrust (T) and drag torque (Q) are generated as the propeller blades rotate through the air, creating a pressure differential. Based on dimensional analysis and Blade Element Momentum Theory (BEMT), the performance is expressed as:

T = Ct · ρ · n2 · D4

Q = Cq · ρ · n2 · D5

Where:

  • ρ is the ambient air density [kg/m3], derived from altitude (H) using the International Standard Atmosphere (ISA) model:

    ρ = ρ0 · (1 - 0.0000225577 · H)4.25588

    (with sea-level density ρ0 = 1.225 kg/m3).
  • n is the rotational speed of the propeller in revolutions per second [rps] (n = RPM / 60).
  • D is the propeller diameter [m].
  • Ct and Cq are the non-dimensional thrust and torque coefficients.

Static Pitch Approximations

In static conditions, the coefficients are heavily dependent on the propeller's pitch-to-diameter ratio (P/D):

Ct_static ≈ 0.115 · (P ⁄ D)

Cq_static ≈ Ct_static · (0.045 · Refactor + 0.11 · (P ⁄ D))

Low Reynolds Number Correction

Small drone propellers operate in a low Reynolds number regime (Re < 150,000) where viscous shear stresses dominate, increasing the profile drag coefficient. We apply a correction factor based on the 1/4-power law:

Refactor = (150,000 ⁄ Re)0.25

Where Re = (ρ · vtip · cmean) ⁄ μ, with vtip = π · n · D representing blade tip speed, cmean representing mean blade chord, and μ representing air dynamic viscosity.

Induced Inflow Correction

As the propeller rotates, it induces a downward vertical velocity (vi) through the actuator disk, reducing the effective angle of attack of the blades. We calculate the induced advance ratio (Ji) and adjust the coefficients:

Ji = √((2 · Ct_static) ⁄ π)

Ct_eff = Ct_static · (1 - Ji)

Cq_eff = Cq_static · (1 + 1.5 · Ji2)

Worked Example — Static Thrust & Torque Coefficients

Take one 5045 propeller (D = 0.127 m, P/D = 0.9, mean chord cmean ≈ 0.1D = 0.0127 m) spinning at nrpm = 12,000 RPM (n = 200 rps) in sea-level air (ρ = 1.225 kg/m3, μ = 1.81 × 10-5 Pa·s). Stepping through the coefficient model of this section:

Quantity Substitution Result
Ct_static 0.115 · 0.9 0.1035
vtip = π · n · D π · 200 · 0.127 79.8 m/s
Re (1.225 · 79.8 · 0.0127) ⁄ (1.81 × 10-5) 6.86 × 104
Refactor (150,000 ⁄ 68,590)0.25 1.216
Cq_static 0.1035 · (0.045 · 1.216 + 0.11 · 0.9) 0.01591
Ji √(2 · 0.1035 ⁄ π) 0.2567
Ct_eff 0.1035 · (1 − 0.2567) 0.07693
Cq_eff 0.01591 · (1 + 1.5 · 0.25672) 0.01748

Substituting the effective coefficients into the BEMT relations:

T = 0.07693 · 1.225 · 2002 · 0.1274 ≈ 0.981 N

Q = 0.01748 · 1.225 · 2002 · 0.1275 ≈ 0.0283 N·m

At 12,000 RPM a single 5045 develops ≈ 0.981 N — just above the 0.957 N per-motor hover requirement from Section 1 — confirming this motor-prop pair reaches hover at a modest throttle with thrust margin to spare.

3. Equivalent Electrical Circuit & Back-EMF

The brushless DC (BLDC) motor acts as an electromechanical transducer. Under steady-state conditions, the electrical loop is represented by an equivalent series circuit containing the battery open-circuit voltage (Voc), system resistances, and the motor's Back-Electromotive Force (Vbemf).

Total Effective Resistance

The total winding resistance (Rm) is modified to include the battery's internal resistance (Rbatt) and the ESC MOSFET resistance (Resc):

Rm_eff = Rmotor + Resc + 4 · Rbatt

  • ESC Resistance: Ranging from 2 mΩ to 3 mΩ per phase depending on thermal cooling and rating.
  • Battery Resistance: Scales with capacity (Cmah) and cell count (S):

    Rbatt_nominal ≈ S · 0.012 · (1500 ⁄ Cmah) Ω

Back-EMF & Applied Voltage

When the motor spins at speed nrpm, it generates a counter-voltage opposing the applied voltage:

Vbemf = nrpm ⁄ KV

Where KV is the motor velocity constant [RPM/V]. The effective voltage applied to the windings by the ESC switching MOSFETs under throttle u ∈ [0, 1] is:

Vapplied = u · Vbattery_terminal

Steady-State Current & Torque

The loop current (I) is driven by the difference between the applied voltage and Back-EMF:

I = (Vapplied - Vbemf) ⁄ Rm_eff

The motor produces torque (Qm) proportional to this current, which must balance the aerodynamic torque (Q) and winding friction losses:

Qm = Kt · (I - I0)

Where Kt = 60 ⁄ (2π · KV) is the motor torque constant [N·m/A], and I0 is the no-load current.

Worked Example — Circuit Operating Point

For the 2204-2300KV motor (KV = 2300 RPM/V, Rmotor = 0.095 Ω, I0 = 0.30 A) on the 4S 2200 mAh pack through a 30 A ESC (Resc = 0.0025 Ω), first build the effective resistance. The pack internal resistance is Rbatt = 4 · 0.012 · (1500 ⁄ 2200) ≈ 0.0327 Ω, so:

Rm_eff = 0.095 + 0.0025 + 4 · 0.0327 ≈ 0.2284 Ω

At the hover speed nrpm = 12,000 RPM the back-EMF is:

Vbemf = 12,000 ⁄ 2300 ≈ 5.22 V

With an applied voltage Vapplied ≈ 6.84 V (throttle u ≈ 0.46 of the 14.8 V terminal), the loop current is:

I = (6.84 − 5.22) ⁄ 0.2284 ≈ 7.12 A

With Kt = 60 ⁄ (2π · 2300) ≈ 0.00415 N·m/A, the motor torque Qm = 0.00415 · (7.12 − 0.30) ≈ 0.0283 N·m, which exactly balances the aerodynamic torque Q computed in Section 2. The 7.12 A draw sits well below the motor's 25 A limit, so this hover operating point is safe.

4. Dynamic Thermal & Convective Cooling Kinetics

The electrical current flowing through the windings generates heat due to resistive losses (Joule heating). The rate of temperature change (dT/dt) of the motor is solved using a first-order lumped capacitance thermal model:

dT ⁄ dt = (Ploss - (T - Tambient) · hcooling) ⁄ (Mmotor · Cp)

Where:

  • Ploss = I2 · Rmotor is the electrical copper loss [W] (iron core losses are neglected for simplicity).
  • T is the current winding temperature [°C], and Tambient is the air temperature (typically 25°C).
  • Mmotor is the physical mass of the motor [kg], and Cp is the specific heat capacity of copper/aluminum (900 J/(kg·K)).
  • hcooling is the dynamic convective heat transfer coefficient [W/K], which scales with the propeller wash velocity and blade tip speed:

    hcooling = 0.025 + 0.006 · √vtip + 0.012 · vi

Worked Example — Steady-State Winding Temperature

Continuing the hover operating point (I = 7.12 A, Rmotor = 0.095 Ω), the copper loss is:

Ploss = 7.122 · 0.095 ≈ 4.81 W

The induced wash speed through the disk (area A = πD2⁄4 = 0.01267 m2) is vi = √(T ⁄ (2ρA)) = √(0.981 ⁄ (2 · 1.225 · 0.01267)) ≈ 5.62 m/s, which with the tip speed vtip = 79.8 m/s gives a cooling coefficient:

hcooling = 0.025 + 0.006 · √79.8 + 0.012 · 5.62 ≈ 0.146 W/K

At thermal equilibrium (dT/dt = 0) the steady temperature rise above ambient is Ploss ⁄ hcooling = 4.81 ⁄ 0.146 ≈ 32.9 °C, so the winding settles near 25 + 32.9 ≈ 57.9 °C — comfortably below the 150 °C insulation limit.

If the motor temperature exceeds 150°C, the wire insulation melts, triggering short circuits, motor burnout, and eventual crash.

5. Battery Voltage Sag & Discharge Dynamics

As a Lithium Polymer (LiPo) battery discharges, its open-circuit voltage (Voc) drops as a function of the State of Charge (SoC ∈ [0, 1]):

Voc_cell = 3.5 + 0.7 · SoC + 0.1 · SoC3

Under high current draw, the terminal voltage drops (sags) below the open-circuit voltage due to internal cell resistance:

Vterminal = Ncells · (Voc_cell - I · Rcell_eff)

Near depletion (SoC < 0.30), chemical reaction rates slow down, causing cell internal resistance to grow exponentially:

Rcell_eff = Rcell_nominal · (1.0 + 0.25 · e5.0 · (0.3 - SoC))

This voltage sag reduces Vapplied, causing a corresponding drop in RPM and thrust, representing battery depletion effects during flight.

Worked Example — Voltage Sag at Hover

At half charge (SoC = 0.5) the per-cell open-circuit voltage of the 4S 2200 mAh pack is Voc_cell = 3.5 + 0.7 · 0.5 + 0.1 · 0.53 ≈ 3.863 V. The per-cell nominal resistance is Rcell_nominal = 0.012 · (1500 ⁄ 2200) ≈ 0.00818 Ω. Since SoC > 0.30 the cell resistance is still near nominal:

Rcell_eff = 0.00818 · (1 + 0.25 · e5.0 · (0.3 − 0.5)) ≈ 0.00893 Ω

Drawing the hover current I = 7.12 A from Section 3:

Vterminal = 4 · (3.863 − 7.12 · 0.00893) ≈ 15.2 V

Near depletion (SoC = 0.2) the same current sees a swollen resistance Rcell_eff ≈ 0.01155 Ω and a lower Voc_cell ≈ 3.641 V, dropping the terminal voltage to ≈ 14.2 V. This ≈ 1 V sag directly lowers Vapplied, forcing a higher throttle to hold the same RPM and thrust as the pack empties.
