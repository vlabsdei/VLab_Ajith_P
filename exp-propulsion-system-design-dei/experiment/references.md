# Academic References

The mathematical models, electrical circuit equations, and aerodynamic parameters implemented in this experiment are derived from the following aerospace, electrical engineering, and battery chemistry literature:

1. **J. G. Leishman**, *Principles of Helicopter Aerodynamics*, 2nd ed. Cambridge, UK: Cambridge University Press, 2006.  
   *(Provides the foundational derivations for Blade Element Momentum Theory (BEMT), actuator disk inflow velocity, and induced advance ratio corrections in hover).*

2. **M. Drela**, "QPROP: Formulation for Second-Order Propeller/Windmill Analysis," MIT Department of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Cambridge, MA, Tech. Rep. MIT-Aero-2007-1, 2007.  
   *(Outlines numerical methods for modeling small UAV-scale propellers operating in low Reynolds number viscous drag regimes).*

3. **D. C. Hanselman**, *Brushless Permanent Magnet Motor Design*, 2nd ed. Madison, WI: Magna Physics Publishing, 2006.  
   *(Covers permanent magnet synchronous motor equivalent circuit kinetics, winding copper losses, Back-EMF constant relationships, and torque-current equations).*

4. **J. D. Anderson, Jr.**, *Introduction to Flight*, 8th ed. New York, NY: McGraw-Hill Education, 2016.  
   *(Details the standard atmospheric model equations for dynamic pressure, dynamic viscosity, and air density scaling with altitude).*

5. **NASA Technical Memorandum 101340**, "Aerodynamic Performance of Small-Scale Propellers at Low Reynolds Numbers," National Aeronautics and Space Administration, Washington, DC, 1989.  
   *(Experimental data validating viscous drag profile corrections and boundary layer transitions for small, light-load propellers).*
