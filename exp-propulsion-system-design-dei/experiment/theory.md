# Theoretical Background: Multirotor Propulsion Systems

A multirotor's propulsion system converts electrical energy stored in a chemical battery into aerodynamic thrust. Designing an efficient and safe drone requires understanding the coupled physics of aerodynamics, electro-circuit kinetics, thermodynamics, and battery chemistry.

---

## **1. Aerodynamic Force Balance & Hover State**

For a multirotor to maintain a stationary hover in three-dimensional space, the net vertical force acting on the aircraft must be zero. The total thrust generated by the <i>N</i><sub>motors</sub> propellers must balance the gravity load (weight) of the drone:

<p align="center">&Sigma; <i>F</i><sub><i>y</i></sub> = <i>N</i><sub>motors</sub> &middot; <i>T</i> - <i>M</i><sub>total</sub> &middot; <i>g</i> = 0</p>

<p align="center"><b><i>T</i><sub>required</sub> = <sup>(<i>M</i><sub>total</sub> &middot; <i>g</i>)</sup> &frasl; <sub><i>N</i><sub>motors</sub></sub></b></p>

Where:
* <i>T</i><sub>required</sub> is the static thrust required per motor-propeller unit [N].
* <i>M</i><sub>total</sub> is the total All-Up Mass (AUM) of the quadcopter, including the frame, electronics, battery, propulsion units, and any payload [kg].
* <i>g</i> is the acceleration due to gravity (9.80665 m/s<sup>2</sup>).
* <i>N</i><sub>motors</sub> is the number of motors (typically 4 for standard quadcopters).

## **2. Propeller Aerodynamics (Blade Element Momentum Theory)**

Aerodynamic thrust (<i>T</i>) and drag torque (<i>Q</i>) are generated as the propeller blades rotate through the air, creating a pressure differential. Based on dimensional analysis and Blade Element Momentum Theory (BEMT), the performance is expressed as:

<p align="center"><i>T</i> = <i>C</i><sub><i>t</i></sub> &middot; &rho; &middot; <i>n</i><sup>2</sup> &middot; <i>D</i><sup>4</sup></p>

<p align="center"><i>Q</i> = <i>C</i><sub><i>q</i></sub> &middot; &rho; &middot; <i>n</i><sup>2</sup> &middot; <i>D</i><sup>5</sup></p>

Where:
* &rho; is the ambient air density [kg/m<sup>3</sup>], derived from altitude (<i>H</i>) using the International Standard Atmosphere (ISA) model:
  <p align="center">&rho; = &rho;<sub>0</sub> &middot; (1 - 0.0000225577 &middot; <i>H</i>)<sup>4.25588</sup></p>
  (with sea-level density &rho;<sub>0</sub> = 1.225 kg/m<sup>3</sup>).
* <i>n</i> is the rotational speed of the propeller in revolutions per second [rps] (<i>n</i> = RPM / 60).
* <i>D</i> is the propeller diameter [m].
* <i>C</i><sub><i>t</i></sub> and <i>C</i><sub><i>q</i></sub> are the non-dimensional thrust and torque coefficients.

The velocity vectors, angles, and resulting incremental aerodynamic forces acting on a 2D airfoil cross-section of the blade are shown below:

![Blade Section Aerodynamics (BEMT)](./images/blade_forces_bemt.png)

### **Static Pitch Approximations**
In static conditions, the coefficients are heavily dependent on the propeller's pitch-to-diameter ratio (<i>P</i>/<i>D</i>):

<p align="center"><i>C</i><sub><i>t</i>_static</sub> &asymp; 0.115 &middot; (<sup><i>P</i></sup> &frasl; <sub><i>D</i></sub>)</p>

<p align="center"><i>C</i><sub><i>q</i>_static</sub> &asymp; <i>C</i><sub><i>t</i>_static</sub> &middot; (0.045 &middot; <i>Re</i><sub>factor</sub> + 0.11 &middot; (<sup><i>P</i></sup> &frasl; <sub><i>D</i></sub>))</p>

### **Low Reynolds Number Correction**
Small drone propellers operate in a low Reynolds number regime (<i>Re</i> &lt; 150,000) where viscous shear stresses dominate, increasing the profile drag coefficient. We apply a correction factor based on the 1/4-power law:

<p align="center"><i>Re</i><sub>factor</sub> = (<sup>150,000</sup> &frasl; <sub><i>Re</i></sub>)<sup>0.25</sup></p>

Where <i>Re</i> = <sup>(&rho; &middot; <i>v</i><sub>tip</sub> &middot; <i>c</i><sub>mean</sub>)</sup> &frasl; <sub>&mu;</sub>, with <i>v</i><sub>tip</sub> = &pi; &middot; <i>n</i> &middot; <i>D</i> representing blade tip speed, <i>c</i><sub>mean</sub> representing mean blade chord, and &mu; representing air dynamic viscosity.

### **Induced Inflow Correction**
As the propeller rotates, it induces a downward vertical velocity (<i>v<sub>i</sub></i>) through the actuator disk, reducing the effective angle of attack of the blades. We calculate the induced advance ratio (<i>J<sub>i</sub></i>) and adjust the coefficients:

<p align="center"><i>J</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> = &radic;(<sup>(2 &middot; <i>C</i><sub><i>t</i>_static</sub>)</sup> &frasl; <sub>&pi;</sub>)</p>

<p align="center"><i>C</i><sub><i>t</i>_eff</sub> = <i>C</i><sub><i>t</i>_static</sub> &middot; (1 - <i>J</i><sub><i>i</i></sub>)</p>

<p align="center"><i>C</i><sub><i>q</i>_eff</sub> = <i>C</i><sub><i>q</i>_static</sub> &middot; (1 + 1.5 &middot; <i>J</i><sub><i>i</i></sub><sup>2</sup>)</p>

## **3. Equivalent Electrical Circuit & Back-EMF**

The brushless DC (BLDC) motor acts as an electromechanical transducer. Under steady-state conditions, the electrical loop is represented by an equivalent series circuit containing the battery open-circuit voltage (<i>V</i><sub>oc</sub>), system resistances, and the motor's Back-Electromotive Force (<i>V</i><sub>bemf</sub>).

![Propulsion Circuit Diagram](./images/propulsion_circuit_diagram.png)

### **Total Effective Resistance**
The total winding resistance (<i>R</i><sub><i>m</i></sub>) is modified to include the battery's internal resistance (<i>R</i><sub>batt</sub>) and the ESC MOSFET resistance (<i>R</i><sub>esc</sub>):

<p align="center"><i>R</i><sub><i>m</i>_eff</sub> = <i>R</i><sub>motor</sub> + <i>R</i><sub>esc</sub> + 4 &middot; <i>R</i><sub>batt</sub></p>

* **ESC Resistance:** Ranging from 2 m&Omega; to 3 m&Omega; per phase depending on thermal cooling and rating.
* **Battery Resistance:** Scales with capacity (<i>C</i><sub>mah</sub>) and cell count (<i>S</i>):
  <p align="center"><i>R</i><sub>batt_nominal</sub> &asymp; <i>S</i> &middot; 0.012 &middot; (<sup>1500</sup> &frasl; <sub><i>C</i><sub>mah</sub></sub>) &Omega;</p>

### **Back-EMF & Applied Voltage**
When the motor spins at speed <i>n</i><sub>rpm</sub>, it generates a counter-voltage opposing the applied voltage:

<p align="center"><i>V</i><sub>bemf</sub> = <sup><i>n</i><sub>rpm</sub></sup> &frasl; <sub><i>KV</i></sub></p>

Where <i>KV</i> is the motor velocity constant [RPM/V].
The effective voltage applied to the windings by the ESC switching MOSFETs under throttle <i>u</i> &isin; [0, 1] is:

<p align="center"><i>V</i><sub>applied</sub> = <i>u</i> &middot; <i>V</i><sub>battery_terminal</sub></p>

### **Steady-State Current & Torque**
The loop current (<i>I</i>) is driven by the difference between the applied voltage and Back-EMF:

<p align="center"><i>I</i> = <sup>(<i>V</i><sub>applied</sub> - <i>V</i><sub>bemf</sub>)</sup> &frasl; <sub><i>R</i><sub><i>m</i>_eff</sub></sub></p>

The motor produces torque (<i>Q</i><sub><i>m</i></sub>) proportional to this current, which must balance the aerodynamic torque (<i>Q</i>) and winding friction losses:

<p align="center"><i>Q</i><sub><i>m</i></sub> = <i>K</i><sub><i>t</i></sub> &middot; (<i>I</i> - <i>I</i><sub>0</sub>)</p>

Where <i>K</i><sub><i>t</i></sub> = <sup>60</sup> &frasl; <sub>(2&pi; &middot; <i>KV</i>)</sub> is the motor torque constant [N·m/A], and <i>I</i><sub>0</sub> is the no-load current.

## **4. Dynamic Thermal & Convective Cooling Kinetics**

The electrical current flowing through the windings generates heat due to resistive losses (Joule heating). The rate of temperature change (<i>dT</i>/<i>dt</i>) of the motor is solved using a first-order lumped capacitance thermal model:

<p align="center"><sup><i>dT</i></sup> &frasl; <sub><i>dt</i></sub> = <sup>(<i>P</i><sub>loss</sub> - (<i>T</i> - <i>T</i><sub>ambient</sub>) &middot; <i>h</i><sub>cooling</sub>)</sup> &frasl; <sub>(<i>M</i><sub>motor</sub> &middot; <i>C</i><sub><i>p</i></sub>)</sub></p>

Where:
* <i>P</i><sub>loss</sub> = <i>I</i><sup>2</sup> &middot; <i>R</i><sub>motor</sub> is the electrical copper loss [W] (iron core losses are neglected for simplicity).
* <i>T</i> is the current winding temperature [°C], and <i>T</i><sub>ambient</sub> is the air temperature (typically 25&deg;C).
* <i>M</i><sub>motor</sub> is the physical mass of the motor [kg], and <i>C</i><sub><i>p</i></sub> is the specific heat capacity of copper/aluminum (900 J/(kg·K)).
* <i>h</i><sub>cooling</sub> is the dynamic convective heat transfer coefficient [W/K], which scales with the propeller wash velocity and blade tip speed:
  <p align="center"><i>h</i><sub>cooling</sub> = 0.025 + 0.006 &middot; &radic;<i>v</i><sub>tip</sub> + 0.012 &middot; <i>v<sub>i</sub></i></p>

If the motor temperature exceeds 150&deg;C, the wire insulation melts, triggering short circuits, motor burnout, and eventual crash.

This heat flow and dissipation behavior can be represented by an equivalent thermal RC circuit, where <i>P</i><sub>loss</sub> acts as a current heat flux source, <i>C</i><sub>th</sub> represents the thermal capacitance (storing heat), and <i>R</i><sub>th</sub> represents the thermal resistance of convective cooling:

## **5. Battery Voltage Sag & Discharge Dynamics**

As a Lithium Polymer (LiPo) battery discharges, its open-circuit voltage (<i>V</i><sub>oc</sub>) drops as a function of the State of Charge (<i>SoC</i> &isin; [0, 1]):

<p align="center"><i>V</i><sub>oc_cell</sub> = 3.5 + 0.7 &middot; <i>SoC</i> + 0.1 &middot; <i>SoC</i><sup>3</sup></p>

Under high current draw, the terminal voltage drops (sags) below the open-circuit voltage due to internal cell resistance:

<p align="center"><i>V</i><sub>terminal</sub> = <i>N</i><sub>cells</sub> &middot; (<i>V</i><sub>oc_cell</sub> - <i>I</i> &middot; <i>R</i><sub>cell_eff</sub>)</p>

Near depletion (<i>SoC</i> &lt; 0.30), chemical reaction rates slow down, causing cell internal resistance to grow exponentially:

<p align="center"><i>R</i><sub>cell_eff</sub> = <i>R</i><sub>cell_nominal</sub> &middot; (1.0 + 0.25 &middot; <i>e</i><sup>5.0 &middot; (0.3 - <i>SoC</i>)</sup>)</p>

This voltage sag reduces <i>V</i><sub>applied</sub>, causing a corresponding drop in RPM and thrust, representing battery depletion effects during flight.
